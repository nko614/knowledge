How Development Works: From Request to Live

Hours of work are not a go-live date

A change estimated at 60 hours usually goes live 5 to 7 weeks after it is approved. The 60 hours are the working hours Odoo spends on the change, including development and testing. They do not count the days the change spends waiting: for answers, for the test week and for the next release date.

The hours tell you what the change costs. The target release tells you when it goes live. Every estimate we send will show both.

The steps every change goes through

Every change, large or small, goes through the same six steps. The times below are typical for a 60-hour change.

StepWhat happensWhoTypical calendar time
1. Agree the requirementsThe analyst writes down exactly what the change must do, and the customer confirms it. Questions are answered here.Customer and Odoo analyst2 to 5 days
2. DevelopmentThe developer writes the change.Odoo developerAbout 2 weeks
3. Code reviewA second developer checks the work, including its effect on speed and on other parts of the system. Anything found is fixed.Odoo developers1 to 3 days
4. Functional testThe analyst tests the change against the written requirements and adds a screenshot to the task.Odoo analyst1 to 2 days
5. Test weekThe change waits for the pre-production lock. Then Odoo tests the full flows and the customer tests their daily processes.Odoo and customer1 to 2 weeks
6. ReleaseThe change goes to production on the next scheduled release date.OdooRelease day

If a test finds a problem, the change goes back to step 2 for that fix and then through the later steps again.

What makes a change go live sooner

  • The request is complete and clear before work starts, so no questions come up during development.
  • The change affects one area, such as a report or a single screen, and does not touch other processes.
  • A developer is free to start right away and can work on it without interruption.
  • Development finishes just before the pre-production lock, so the change does not wait long for the test week.
  • Testing finds no problems, so nothing goes back for rework.

What makes a change take longer

  • Questions come up during development and the work stops until they are answered.
  • The request changes after work has started. Even a small change in what is wanted can mean redoing work already finished.
  • The change touches checkout, payments, stock or an integration. These areas affect many other processes, so they need more review and more testing.
  • The change depends on another company, such as a payment provider, shipping carrier or marketplace. We have to wait for their access, documentation or answers.
  • Testing in step 4 or step 5 finds a problem, and the change goes back to development.
  • During the test week, the customer finds that they expected something that was not in the written requirements. That becomes extra work or a new request.
  • An urgent problem in production takes the developer away from the change.
  • The change misses the pre-production lock and waits for the following release.

Why more developers do not always make it faster

Most of a change has to be built in order, because each part depends on the one before it. Two developers cannot write the same piece at once. A developer new to the change also needs time to learn how it works before they can help.

The steps after development do not get shorter either. Review, testing and the release date stay the same however many people built the change.

How we will tell you when a change goes live

Each estimate will show two things:

  • Hours: the work Odoo does on the change, including development and testing, which is what the change costs.
  • Target release: the release date we are aiming for, which is when the change goes live.

If something moves the target release, we will update the task with the new release and the reason.

The customer can help a change go live sooner by:

  • Describing everything the change must do in the request, including related behavior they expect.
  • Answering questions within one to two working days.
  • Keeping the request the same once development has started, and sending new ideas as new requests.
  • Testing during the test week so the change can be approved on time.
