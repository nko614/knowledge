How Development Works: From Request to Live

Estimated hours and the go-live date

The 60 hours in an estimate are the time Odoo's team spends working on the change. They do not count the days the change spends waiting: for answers, for the test week and for the release date. How long a change takes from approval to going live depends mostly on how long it waits for the next test week.

The steps every change goes through

Every change goes through the same six steps. The times below are typical for a 60-hour change, in working days. A critical fix for a problem in the live system can be released outside the schedule, but it is still reviewed and tested.

If a test finds a problem, the developer fixes it and the fix is tested again. If the change is not approved by the end of the test week, it moves to the next release.

Example: a 60-hour change

Odoo's 60 hours of work are spread across these steps. Much of the calendar time is spent waiting between them. In this example the change is approved on day 0 and goes live on working day 30, six weeks later.

The example waits 5 days for the test week. A change that is ready just after a test week has started waits for the next one. That wait can be up to 2 weeks with a release every two weeks, and up to 4 weeks with a release every month.

What makes a change go live sooner

  • The request is complete and clear before work starts, so no questions come up during development.
  • The change affects one area, such as a report or a single screen, and does not touch other processes.
  • A developer is free to start right away and can work on it without interruption.
  • Development finishes just before a test week starts, so the change does not wait long for testing.
  • Testing finds no problems, so nothing goes back for rework.

What makes a change take longer

  • Questions come up during development and the work stops until they are answered.
  • The request changes after work has started. Even a small change in what is wanted can mean redoing work already finished.
  • The change touches checkout, payments, stock or an integration. These areas affect many other processes, so they need more review and more testing.
  • The change depends on another company, such as a payment provider, shipping carrier or marketplace. We have to wait for their access, documentation or answers.
  • Testing in step 4 or step 5 finds a problem, and the change goes back to development.
  • During the test week, the customer finds that they expected something that was not in the written requirements. That becomes extra work or a new request.
  • An urgent problem in production takes the developer away from the change.
  • The change is not ready when the test week starts, so it waits for the following release.

Why more developers do not always make it faster

Most of a change has to be built in order, because each part depends on the one before it. Two developers cannot write the same piece at once. A developer new to the change also needs time to learn how it works before they can help.

Adding developers also does not shorten review, testing or the wait for the release date.

What each estimate tells you

Every estimate gives you two numbers:

  • Hours: the time Odoo's team spends working on the change. This is what you pay for.
  • Release date: the date the change is planned to go live.

If the release date changes, we will tell you the new date and the reason in the task.

How you can help a change go live sooner

Several steps depend on your team. Work stops while we wait for a requirement, an answer or a test result, so the speed of these steps sets how soon a change can go live.

  • Describe the change completely in the request: what it must do, who will use it and how you will check that it works. Include anything related you expect to change with it, such as a report or an email.
  • Answer our questions and emails within one to two working days.
  • Once development has started, send new ideas as a new request instead of changing the current one.
  • During the test week, test your full daily processes from start to finish, not only the screen that changed.
  • Report each problem as soon as you find it, with the steps that cause it. When testing is complete, confirm your approval the same day.
